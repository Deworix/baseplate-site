Baseplate vs Ropilot: choose the workflow that fits your game

Both connect AI to Roblox Studio, work with existing games and offer playtesting. Both support using your own AI account. Compare the workflow and total cost on a task from your own project.

Written by the Baseplate team. Competitor details below come from public product pages, not a hands-on benchmark. Pricing can change.

Cost and setup

Ropilot’s pricing page offers built-in AI credits and Bring Your Own AI plans. Its paid BYO Starter plan lists $20/month, plus your separate Claude or ChatGPT account. Ropilot also advertises free starter credits for its built-in AI option.

Baseplate uses your supported agent CLI account. Free is $0, Pro is $4.99/month, and Studio is $12.99/month. Baseplate adds no per-prompt or per-action charge. Your AI provider’s limits and charges still apply.

When to try Baseplate

You already use an AI coding agent, keep your game’s scripts as files, and want tasks, Studio feedback and repeatable tests in one workspace. Start with one small update on Free. A runtime PASS covers the checks performed; it does not prove every mechanic or security property is correct.

When to try Ropilot

You want a bundled AI option without setting up a separate provider account, or prefer its Studio-focused editing and playtesting workflow.

Use the same task to compare

On copies of your game, add one shop item, test its price and inventory change, then check saving. Compare setup time, manual corrections, test evidence and total cost. Neither a feature list nor a clean console settles which tool works better for you.
